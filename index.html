The Story

Built by a learner,
for learners.

"I'm a senior software engineer transitioning into nursing school with the goal of becoming a CRNA. I built HemoLab to learn hemodynamics the way I learn everything else — by building something."

— James Waller, Software Engineer → Nursing Student → Future CRNA

Most clinical education tools are locked behind institutional licenses, built by committees, and designed to assess rather than teach. HemoLab is different: it's free, open source, and built by someone who is actively learning the same material.

Every lesson teaches the concept in plain language first — no assumed knowledge, no glossary required. Then the simulation makes it tangible. You read about MAP = CO × SVR and the equation responds to your input as you read. You load a septic shock scenario and watch MAP collapse as SVR crashes. You apply norepinephrine and watch it recover.

The lesson and the simulation are inseparable. That's the whole idea.

Who it's for

Built for three audiences.
Free for all of them.

Nursing Students

Learning the foundations

Taking A&P for the first time? HemoLab explains hemodynamics the way a good textbook module does — every term defined in context, no external glossary, concepts built on each other. The simulator makes the equations real.

ICU Nurses

Deepening clinical reasoning

You see the numbers on the monitor every shift. HemoLab helps you understand the relationships behind them — why MAP drops in sepsis even when CO is high, when fluid helps and when it doesn't, what each vasoactive drug is actually doing.

CRNA Candidates

Thinking like an anesthetist

Hemodynamic management is the core of anesthesia practice. HemoLab builds the foundation — MAP, cardiac output, the Frank-Starling curve, drug mechanisms — so you arrive at CRNA school with more than memorized facts.
